EU energy transition analysis

Renewable share nearly tripled, yet import dependency fell only 1.4 points. Ridge/Lasso panel models and a Power BI dashboard explain why.

Download the Power BI file Streamlit app Code on GitHub

Streamlit app: free-tier app, may take about 30 seconds to wake up.

Dashboard tour

The five pages

The question

The EU has built a lot of renewable energy since 2005. Has that made it less dependent on imported energy? And what drives how fast each country's renewable share grows?

The result

Although the EU's renewable share nearly tripled (7.2% to 19.8%) over 19 years, energy import dependency fell by only 1.4 percentage points, because dependency also depends on nuclear policy, domestic production and total demand.

A 27-country panel built from the Eurostat REST API was used to train regularised Ridge/Lasso models with expanding-window cross-validation: electricity price was the strongest positive driver of renewable share, and GDP growth was negatively associated with it within countries.

Delivered as six Jupyter notebooks, a five-page Power BI dashboard (DAX measures, synchronised slicers) and an interactive Streamlit app.

Tools

  • Python, Pandas and SQL: cleaning and the 27-country panel
  • scikit-learn: Ridge/Lasso models with expanding-window cross-validation
  • Plotly and Streamlit: the interactive app; Power BI (DAX): the five-page dashboard
  • Data: Eurostat API: energy balances (nrg_bal_c), GDP per capita (nama_10_pc), household electricity prices (nrg_pc_204)
  • Hosting: Streamlit Community Cloud (the Power BI file is in the repo)
