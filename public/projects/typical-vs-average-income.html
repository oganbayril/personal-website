Typical vs average income map

Maps how far the typical person's income sits from the average across 45 countries, 2002-2024. In Türkiye it's about 70%, in most of Europe 83-93%.

Live demo Code on GitHub

The question

The average (mean) income is pulled up by the highest incomes; the median is the income of the person in the middle. How far does the typical person's income sit from the average, and how does that differ across countries and over time?

The measure is the median-to-mean household income ratio: "the typical person has X% of the average income".

The result

An interactive map across 45 European and OECD countries, 2002-2024: in Türkiye the typical person has about 70% of the average income, versus 83-93% in most of Europe.

Eurostat is the default source and OECD is used only to fill gaps (hatched on the map) instead of averaging them. The difference between the two sources was measured on 433 country-years (95% within ±0.03), and that uncertainty is on the page.

Colour tiers sit at natural gaps in the data, and a continuous colour view means the story does not depend on where the lines fall. Suspicious series (Slovakia, North Macedonia, Türkiye) are logged instead of smoothed.

Tools

  • Python and Pandas: data collection, cleaning and source checks
  • Plotly: the interactive map
  • Data: Eurostat EU-SILC (ilc_di03), with the OECD Income Distribution Database filling gaps
  • Hosting: my own VPS with HTTPS
