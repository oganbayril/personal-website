Food and housing spending share

How much of household spending goes to food, housing and utilities across 48 countries, with data-quality decisions documented.

Live demo Code on GitHub

The question

What share of household consumption spending goes to food, housing and utilities, and how does that compare across countries and over time?

Countries are compared against fixed thresholds, the same for every country and year. This measures a share of spending, not of income: it is not an affordability measure.

The result

An interactive map of 49 OECD and European countries (48 on the map), 1995-2024. In 2024, of 44 countries with data, 14 are in the lower tier (below 35%), 27 moderate and 3 higher (45% or more: Czechia, Slovakia, Romania).

Across countries, the food share falls steeply as GDP per capita rises (rank correlation -0.92 in 2024) while the housing share rises (+0.50). Tiers are robust to the classification choice: 93.7% of country-years with data in both COICOP versions keep the same tier.

The thresholds and checks were written down and committed before the share was computed for any country. Every data-quality decision, such as one source and one classification version per country and how imputed rent is handled, is logged with its reason in the repo.

Tools

  • Python and Pandas: loading, combining and checking the sources
  • Plotly: the interactive map
  • Data: Eurostat household spending by purpose, OECD national accounts (non-European OECD members and the UK), World Bank GDP per capita for context
  • Hosting: my own VPS
